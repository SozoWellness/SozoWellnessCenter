# The Technology Behind Cellsonic

Cellsonic is a non-invasive approach that delivers targeted pressure pulses from outside the body. If you’re exploring options for pain, movement or recovery, understanding the technology can help you decide what to ask before a session.

## Pressure Pulses and Your Body’s Repair Processes

Extracorporeal shock wave therapy, or ESWT, uses acoustic pressure waves directed at a targeted area. The aim is to stimulate the body’s natural repair processes rather than introduce a drug or make a surgical incision.

The uploaded source discusses research on ESWT across conditions such as tennis elbow, plantar fasciitis, shoulder tendinopathy and Achilles tendon problems. That research covers different devices and protocols; it does not mean every result applies specifically to Cellsonic or to every patient.

## How ESWT Is Thought to Work

### Blood Flow and Circulation

ESWT research describes changes in local blood flow and signals involved in the formation of new blood vessels. Better circulation can help deliver oxygen and nutrients to tissues involved in repair.

### Pain Signaling

The source describes research into substance P, a chemical involved in pain transmission, and changes in inflammatory mediators. These are proposed explanations for pain reduction following some ESWT protocols.

### Tissue Repair

The source also discusses cellular responses, growth factors and collagen formation. Collagen is a structural component of connective tissues, including tendons. These processes help explain why shockwave approaches are studied for persistent tendon problems.

### Nitric Oxide

Nitric oxide helps blood vessels relax and widen. Research discussed in the source links it with circulation and pain-related responses following ESWT.

## Where Cellsonic Fits

The manufacturer describes Cellsonic as combining very intense pressure pulses with a brief high-voltage electromagnetic field. This differs from the way radial pressure-wave devices generate and deliver their pulses.

At SOZO, we offer both Radial ESWT and Cellsonic ESWT for deeper pain concerns. We can explain the differences and discuss which option may suit your treatment area and goals. The choice is personal—not simply a matter of selecting the strongest setting.

## Why Consider a Non-Invasive Option?

### No Surgical Incision

The treatment head is applied from outside the body, with gel used for transmission. Sessions do not involve an incision. This can make the experience less disruptive than a surgical procedure, although it is not a replacement for surgery when surgery is medically necessary.

### A Drug-Free Treatment Approach

The pulses are intended to stimulate a tissue response without administering a pharmaceutical treatment. This does not mean you should stop prescribed medication; your existing care remains part of the conversation.

### Returning to Your Routine

Many people consider ESWT because it can fit around everyday life. The advice you receive after a session depends on your condition, the treatment area and how you respond. Ask when you can return to exercise and whether any activity should be limited.

## What Might a Session Feel Like?

You may feel tapping, pulsing or discomfort in the treatment area. Your provider should explain the setup, discuss your comfort and review how you respond.

Bruising, swelling and temporary soreness are reported with ESWT. The source describes these effects as often mild and short-lived, but experiences vary. Ask about risks and suitability rather than assuming that non-invasive means risk-free.

## Planning Your Care

### Your Appointment Schedule

Treatment schedules and settings depend on the device, condition and individual. Ask SOZO about the expected appointment length, number of sessions and how progress will be assessed. A protocol described in general ESWT research is not automatically the right protocol for you.

### Understanding the Cost

Before starting, ask for the cost per session, any package options and what follow-up is included. Prices quoted in general industry articles are not SOZO’s prices, and insurance coverage should be confirmed directly with your insurer.

### Questions Worth Asking

- Is my concern appropriate for Radial ESWT or Cellsonic?
- What improvement are we aiming for, and how will we measure it?
- What evidence supports the recommended approach for my condition?
- What side effects or activity restrictions should I expect?
- How does treatment fit with physical therapy or other care I already receive?
