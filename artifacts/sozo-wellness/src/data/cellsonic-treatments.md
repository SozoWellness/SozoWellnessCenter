# Cellsonic Treatments: A Patient’s Guide

This guide covers Cellsonic applications that healthcare providers have reported using in their practices. Cellsonic combines pressure pulses with high-voltage electromagnetic fields. Understanding the approach can help you ask better questions about whether a session fits your needs. Results can vary between patients.

## What Cellsonic Technology Is

Cellsonic combines pressure pulses with powerful electromagnetic fields. The technology developed from the principles behind kidney stone lithotripsy, with the addition of high-voltage electromagnetic pulses.

**How Cellsonic Works:** According to the manufacturer, the technology generates “very intense pressure pulses” by flashing 25,000 volts across electrodes in a water-filled shock head. This creates pressure waves and a brief electromagnetic field. The manufacturer describes this dual-action approach as affecting cellular voltage and electrical properties.

**What You Can Expect:** The technology uses gel for transmission, similar to ultrasound. A treatment head is applied to the targeted area, delivering pulses from outside the body. Your provider determines the treatment area and settings after reviewing your needs.

## Applications You May Hear About

### Orthopedic and Sports Injuries

**Bone Healing:** The source describes practitioner reports involving non-union fractures and delayed-healing bones. These conditions require medical assessment; your orthopedic specialist should guide decisions about your care.

**Soft Tissue and Sports Injuries:**

- Tennis elbow and repetitive strain injuries
- Frozen shoulder and joint mobility issues
- Achilles tendon problems
- Back pain
- Recovery following athletic injuries

If pain or restricted movement is keeping you from the activities you enjoy, ask which approach is appropriate for your diagnosis and recovery goals.

### Pain Management

**Comfort and Function:** Practitioners report using pressure-pulse approaches for persistent pain. The source also discusses proposed effects on nerve voltage and conductivity. Reports of nerve repair, including spinal cord applications, should not be confused with established outcomes or a guarantee of recovery.

**Chronic Pain:** If previous treatments have not brought the relief you hoped for, a conversation about your symptoms, diagnosis and existing care can help determine whether Cellsonic belongs in your plan.

### Wound Care and Infection Management

The source describes practitioner reports involving diabetic foot ulcers, chronic non-healing wounds, infected ulcers, traumatic wounds, burns, and arterial or venous ulcers. It also proposes a mechanical effect on microorganisms.

These reports do not establish Cellsonic as a replacement for wound care, antibiotics, vascular treatment or surgery. A worsening wound, spreading infection or suspected gangrene requires urgent medical care. Do not delay that care while considering a wellness appointment.

### Circulation

**Angiogenesis and Circulation:** The source discusses proposed blood-vessel formation and practitioner reports of circulation changes in people with diabetes or peripheral artery disease. Ask your medical provider what evidence applies to your condition and whether any complementary treatment is appropriate.

### Urological and Emerging Applications

The source also describes reports concerning kidney stones, kidney function, erectile dysfunction and prostate-related concerns. It mentions experimental use relating to pancreatic insulin production.

These are not a list of services confirmed to be available at SOZO, and they should not be treated as established indications for this device. Keep specialist care and prescribed diabetes treatment in place, and discuss any proposed addition with your medical provider.

## What Makes the Technology Different?

### Pressure Pulses and Electromagnetic Fields

The manufacturer describes a high-voltage electromagnetic field generated simultaneously with pressure pulses. Proposed effects include changes in cellular electrical properties, cell membrane permeability and tissue responses.

Different shockwave devices have different characteristics. Evidence for one device or treatment protocol cannot automatically establish the results you can expect from another.

### A Non-Invasive Approach

Cellsonic delivers pulses from outside the body rather than through a surgical incision. Gel helps transmit the pulses to the treatment area. Your comfort, the area being treated and your medical history help guide the session.

## Before Your Appointment

### Share Your Health History

Tell your provider about pregnancy, implanted cardiac devices such as pacemakers, current medications, circulation problems and any existing diagnosis affecting the treatment area. Screening matters even when an approach is non-invasive.

### Ask About Your Treatment Plan

- What is the goal for my treatment area?
- What evidence supports this use of Cellsonic?
- What might I feel during and after a session?
- How many appointments might be considered, and how will progress be reviewed?
- What are the costs, possible side effects and alternatives?
- How should sessions fit alongside my existing medical care?
